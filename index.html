How Auto Loan & Personal Loan EMIs Are Calculated

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified date each calendar month. EMIs are applied to both car loans, bike loans, and personal loans to clear the principal and interest over a specified tenure.

Standard EMI Calculation Formula

EMIs are calculated using the standard financial formula: EMI = [P x R x (1+R)^N] / [(1+R)^N - 1] where P is the Principal loan amount, R is the monthly interest rate, and N is the tenure in months.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the difference between Auto Loan and Personal Loan interest rates?

Auto loans are secured loans backed by the vehicle itself, usually offering lower interest rates (ranging from 8% to 11%). Personal loans are unsecured loans with higher risk for banks, leading to interest rates between 10.5% and 24%.

How does down payment affect my Car Loan EMI?

A higher down payment reduces the net principal loan amount required from the bank, directly lowering both your monthly EMI and total interest burden.

Can I pre-pay or foreclose my loan early?

Yes. Most banks allow foreclosure or partial pre-payment after completing 6 to 12 EMIs, though floating-rate loans typically have zero foreclosure charges compared to fixed-rate personal loans.
